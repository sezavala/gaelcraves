Step 1

Pick a direction

You picked A. The site now opens with people, then food, sells the high-protein angle, and lines items up side by side like Thunderbuns. The other directions stay here for ideas.

A. Smash cam

Picked

The whole first screen is a slow-motion smash with giant type on top. The video sells the food before anyone reads a word.

  • Thunderbuns: Dutch smash burger chain. Video hero, one short line ("Burgers that hit like lightning"), and an Order button repeated about five times down the page.
  • Tacombi: background video loop with a pause button, plus hand-drawn illustrations. "Come for tacos. Stay for the vibe."
  • Graza: olive oil brand with constant playful motion and a pause-animations control. Good model for tone in product names.

B. Back of the house

Option

Lean into the hidden-kitchen story. The menu is printed kitchen tickets, the type looks like a receipt printer, and the walk-in video takes you from Main St to the window.

  • High Street Deli: nostalgic, oversized sandwich photos and a busy neighborhood-shop layout.
  • Loro: unusual type, illustrations and animation, with sidebar navigation.
  • Mighty Fine Burgers: bold red header, retro type, huge burger photos.

C. Street poster

Option

Loud and hand-made: spray paint, stickers, cut-out burgers, jokes in the copy. Feels like a flyer stapled to a telephone pole.

  • Dave's Hot Chicken: red graffiti hero, black base, ORDER NOW under every slide, emoji in headlines.
  • Liquid Death: all-caps jokes ("Murder your thirst") on a horror theme. Proof that a voice can be the design.
  • Streetbird: strong contrast and playful graphics for a chicken spot.

D. Matchday

Option

The taproom fills up for Mexico and USA games, so dress the site like a match program: menu as a team lineup, hours as a scoreboard, a schedule of upcoming watch parties.

  • Ender: Argentine smash burger shop with merch and its own party nights ("Ender Club"). Built in Framer.
  • Ghetto Gastro: culture first, food second, with its own "television" video section.
  • Ma'ono Fried Chicken: bright yellow header and an oversized logo. Shows how far one loud color can carry a small brand.

More to browse: Create Today food gallery (has several food trucks and smash burger shops) and Site Builder Report restaurant list.

Step 2

Camera setup

Frame rate

Shoot everything at 60p. On the computer, slowing a 60p clip to 30 fps gives smooth half-speed slow motion for the smash, cheese pulls and sauce pours. Regular-speed clips just get exported at 30 fps.

Shutter

1/120 at 60p. That keeps motion crisp for slow motion and matches US 60 Hz lights, so there's no flicker. You may need to raise ISO or add the LED light.

Color

Use Standard or S-Cinetone. Skip S-Log unless you plan to color grade. Set white balance off a white plate under the kitchen lights.

Framing

Shoot horizontal, but keep the food in the middle third. That lets one clip be cropped to vertical for phones and Reels.

Stillness

Use a tripod for every loop. A clip that moves can't loop cleanly. Roll 15 to 20 seconds to get one clean 6 to 10 second loop.

Light

Put a small LED light behind the griddle, aimed toward the lens. Steam and smoke light up and look great on video.

Protect the camera

Grease spits. Stay 2 to 3 feet back and zoom in, and wipe the lens between shots.

Sound

Leave the mic on. The site plays clips muted, but the sizzle is gold for Instagram.
